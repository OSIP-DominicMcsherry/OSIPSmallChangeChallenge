One Small Change

Stop trying to fix your whole life. Start with one thing.

You do not need to fix everything. You need to start paying attention to what actually needs you. Part one

Choose your area

Before changing anything, look honestly at where you actually are. You cannot meaningfully respond to what you have not first been willing to see clearly.

OSIP says

Seeing where you are is not the same as accepting it. You can look straight at something and still resist it, grieve it, or work to change it. Seeing does not tell you what to do. It makes it possible to respond to the actual thing rather than the version you edited for comfort.

Try this

Read all eleven areas below. Notice which one you keep returning to, and be honest rather than picking the one you think you should pick. One area is the whole point. Two is allowed if you genuinely cannot separate them, but one will always work better.

Part two

Write your action

Something you could do today, tomorrow, and on the worst day of your week.

OSIP says

Make it almost too easy. This is not testing how much you can endure, it is testing whether you can keep a promise to yourself. If the action feels impressive, it is probably too big.

Try this

Write it as a specific sentence with a time attached. Not I need to exercise more, but I will walk for twenty minutes after work. Then shrink it until it is boring.

Part four

Eight days, one action

Day 0 lands the day before we start. Days 1 to 7 run Monday to Sunday. Each day adds a layer to the same single action and never asks you to add a second one. Everything you write saves as you type.

DAY 0Begin with realitySUN

What area of my life currently needs the most attention?

This is Part one above. Give it longer than feels necessary. Not the area that would look good, and not the one you think you should pick. The one that is actually asking for something.

DAY 1Choose your actionMON

What is the one small thing I am willing to do every day this week?

Turn the area into something concrete, which is Part two above. Then ask yourself honestly why you want this change, and whether the answer holds up. Changes rooted in something you actually value hold better than changes you are doing because you feel you should.

DAY 2Show upTUE

Did I do what I said I would do?

OSIP says

Motivation is not the goal. Showing up is.

You will not feel like it every day this week, and that is not a sign anything is wrong. When the resistance turns up, work out what kind it is. Tired, afraid, bored, genuinely overloaded, or avoiding discomfort. Sometimes the right answer is to rest and sometimes it is to push through, and telling the difference is a skill rather than an instinct.

DAY 3NoticeWED

What did I notice when I actually slowed down enough to pay attention?

The question is not whether you succeeded, it is what this information is teaching you. What surfaces is rarely about the action itself. It sounds more like realising you are more tired than you thought, or that you are on your phone far more than you knew, or that you never talk to anyone about what is going on, or that you are much harder on yourself than you would be on a friend.

DAY 4What is yours to carryTHU

What is actually within my control here?

Life gets lighter when you stop carrying what was never yours, and stronger when you willingly carry what is. Not everything that happens is your responsibility, but something almost always is. Your actions, your choices, how you communicate, your boundaries, your willingness to try again or to ask for help.

Try this

Put your effort into the first column deliberately, then notice what happens to the second one when you stop feeding it.

DAY 5Nothing stands aloneFRI

What else might be connected to the thing I am trying to change?

You are a system, not a set of separate categories. Sleep affects mood, mood affects relationships, relationships affect belonging, belonging affects motivation. Someone chooses exercise and finds they are exhausted because they are sleeping badly. Someone chooses nutrition and finds the eating follows the stress. Someone chooses discipline and finds they are lonely.

Try this

Ask the second question as well, which is usually the more useful one. Which area, if it improved, would probably lift several others with it?

DAY 6Human before perfectSAT
OSIP says

A missed day is data, not failure. You are not starting again, you are continuing. No restarting, no guilt, no going back to day one.

By now some of you will have missed a day, and that is part of the week rather than a break in it. That cycle of going hard, slipping, feeling terrible and starting over on Monday is the thing that has been keeping you stuck. It was never your discipline.

The position here is not be kind to yourself so you never have to change. It is treat yourself like a human being while you change. Responsibility does not require cruelty, and compassion does not require abandoning responsibility.

DAY 7What will you carry forwardSUN

Three questions, and they are worth twenty unhurried minutes. This is the most valuable thing you will produce all week.

Send my reflection to Dom
OSIP says

Do not add five more habits. Choose the one thing worth carrying forward.

You can change the plan without calling it failure. Make it smaller, make it easier, make it more enjoyable, change your environment, ask for support, or decide the goal itself was the wrong one. All of that is adjustment, and adjustment is part of the method rather than a retreat from it.

Part five

What you are actually running

This week is one turn of the OSIP loop, shortened to seven days so you can feel the shape of it.

Reality→ Understanding→ Responsibility→ Action→ Reflection→ Adjustment→ and round again
OSIP says

Struggle is not the enemy. Avoidance is. It is a loop rather than a staircase, and there is no final version of it. That is the point. The question underneath all of this is simply whether you are participating in your life.

Part six

The Practical Actions Menu

Yours free with the challenge. Eleven areas of life and a menu of realistic actions under each one. It is the engine behind this whole week and it stays useful long after the seven days are over. These are not rules and not a prescription, they are ideas worth exploring.

Part seven

Keep going

You do not need another challenge. You need to keep living what you have learned.

Seven days does not change a life. It does something more useful than that. It shows you how you actually respond when you make a small promise to yourself and try to keep it, and that is the thing worth building on.

The challenge finishes on Sunday. We will see what we have learned and decide where to go from there.
